    Background: High-Stakes Alerts for Smart Barns

    For Precision Livestock Diagnostics (PLD), notifications were critical to preventing mass animal casualties and catastrophic property damage. The PLD system relied on an intricate system of sensors tracking livestock health alongside complex water, feed, and silo infrastructure. In this high-risk environment alerts demanded careful design and development roadmaps.

    As Project Manager of PLD Development, I worked with PLD stakeholders and Tandem’s Development team to plan and support implementation of 15+ high-priority alerts for the PLD system.

    Design: How the Worksheet was Created

    When we began architecting the notifications, Systems Design professional James Ritchie and I were primarily using our past experience and online research. The high-risk nature of the PLD system demanded a more rigorous approach. In our discovery sessions we quickly realized the existing industry research and documentation lacked the depth required for such complex environments.

    Leveraging my UX Research and Design background, I began systematically tracking our major technical decision points. Eventually distilling months of stakeholder conversations, developer debates, and academic perspectives into a unified framework. The worksheet proved so impactful it is a permanent fixture in Tandem’s product design workflow.

    I created this worksheet to help businesses make intentional notifications, respecting user focus while reliably delivering crucial information.

    Impact: Streamlining Product Delivery

    • Evaluate notification viability: Assessing whether the alert meets user and business requirements enough to justify engineering resources.
    • Inform Sprint Planning: Pinpoint where further research is required to avoid costly development mistakes.
    • Elevate Stakeholder Conversations: Outlining complex notification mechanics to facilitate strategic product choices.
    • Align Cross-Functional Communication: Demystifying technical roadblocks through a shared vision among developers, designers, and business stakeholders.
    • Empower Stakeholders: Providing stakeholders the vocabulary and confidence to pitch technical systems to prospective clients.
