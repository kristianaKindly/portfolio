Designing the Right Tree Structure

A comprehensive guide on designing friction-free tree structures.

On this page

    A Tree Structure organizes objects and information hierarchically, the best known examples are the folders and files structures on a computer desktop.

    Background: Untangling 20 Years of Legacy Architecture

    As lead UX Designer I was tasked to design and wireframe the rebuild of a 20 year old travel enterprise system. The platform managed everything from high-level logistics, like mapping out plane and bus seating layouts, to ground-level details like hotel configuration, room inventory and cleaning schedules. Everything within the system was organized using numerous types of labyrinthian tree structures.

    For the travel enterprise group (and the vast majority of systems), the tree structures mapped both real-world structures (planes, buses, etc.) and the operational backbone of the company’s daily logistics. Dispatchers spent their shifts navigating these hierarchies, buried information frequently causing logistical bottlenecks, stalling high-stakes tasks and resulting in severe, real-world operational consequences.

    Design: How the Flowchart was Created

    This guide was developed over the course of designing this system, a major foundational resource was Interaction Designer Hagan Rivers’s wonderful article “Interaction Design for Trees” (I list several other references below).

    I synthesized my experiences creating this system with conversations and feedback from Tandem developers and Designers. I found it valuable to include development considerations into the guide, considering what UI elements are often easily implemented components that don’t require custom development.

    Impact: Tree Structure that Stand the Test of Time

    • Improve Spatial Awareness and Process Tracking: Understanding the pros and cons of each tree type, when it’s a good idea to focus on clarity versus depth of information, ensuring the tree matches the user intent.
    • Prevent Costly Development Rework: Eliminating guess-work by presenting in-depth breakdown of each type of tree structure. With better information teams can negotiate which tree structure will work best, rather than developing a system they hope will work.
    • Ensure Long-Term Scalability: Protecting software from turning into another rigid legacy system, knowing what you’re working with beforehand.
